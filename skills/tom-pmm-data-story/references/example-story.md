# Example: the Fabrikam Desk data story (fictional)

**Everything in this example is fictional:** Example Feedback, the help desk tool Fabrikam Desk, every customer, person, quote, number and link. It shows the format to aspire to. In practice, a story like this is drafted by Claude from the Hex numbers, then reshaped by the marketing team in a doc. Read it before drafting any data story, and copy its shape, not its sentences.

## What makes it work

- **Fact-first title with a hook as the tail.** "How Fabrikam Desk customers turn 1.2M+ tickets and 300K+ survey responses into feedback that fixes itself, using Example Feedback." Numbers, the partner, your product, and an action verb. Never hook-first, never "centralize", never a word that sounds like storage.
- **Platform framing before the numbers.** Two sentences that say what the product is (a Voice of Customer platform) and how the integration runs in both directions (feedback out, feedback in). A cold reader needs this before any stat means anything.
- **A method sentence.** "We looked at how Example Feedback's Fabrikam Desk customers use the integration, with a focus on our B2C software and apps segment, and at what scale." It tells the reader whose numbers these are and over what window.
- **The stat strip carries the conceptual frame.** Each cell is labeled with the idea it proves (the inner loop, the outer loop, the channels), so the strip teaches the reader the vocabulary the story will use.
- **A framework table before the use cases.** Inner loop and outer loop in a three-column table (Loop / What it fixes / Where it runs), then one payoff line. CX readers already use these terms; the table makes them concrete for everyone else.
- **A use-case table with the numbers in it.** Use case / Loop / What happens / Last 12 months. Three rows, every number exact, overlap stated once underneath so nobody adds them.
- **Social proof from the quote library, not a protagonist.** Two customer quotes pulled from the Notion quote library with full attribution. They prove the pattern is real without making the piece about one customer.
- **Problem, shift, then mechanics.** The classic customer story arc: a problem the reader recognizes, the turning point, then how each use case works with a screenshot per use case.
- **Vertical callouts as percentages of the parent block**, never raw numbers, under one easy-reading label ("B2C software and apps").
- **Setup links mapped to use cases**, so Sales, CS and partners can forward it.
- **Closes on the outer loop and the proof line** ("the most-used help desk integration on Example Feedback, at 2.1x the next one").

Still open on this version when it was captured: the word "centralize" (action language is preferred instead), and the three `{screenshot}` placeholders.

# How Fabrikam Desk customers turn 1.2M+ tickets and 300K+ survey responses into feedback that fixes itself, using Example Feedback.

January 15, 2026 · Sam Rivera

**Feature:** Fabrikam Desk integration · **Built for:** CX Manager, Head of Customer Experience · **Cohort:** B2C software and apps · **Data window:** January 2025 to December 2025

Every CX team measures satisfaction across the journey. Far fewer close the loop on a single bad score.

Example Feedback is a Voice of Customer platform that integrates with Fabrikam Desk in both directions. **Feedback out:** an Example Feedback survey goes to the customer from Fabrikam Desk when a ticket closes, and the answer goes back to the ticket. **Feedback in:** Fabrikam Desk tickets flow into Example Feedback, centralized next to survey responses and 10+ other sources of feedback.

We looked at how Example Feedback's Fabrikam Desk customers use the integration, with a focus on our B2C software and apps segment, and at what scale. The numbers below cover all Fabrikam Desk customers from January 2025 to December 2025.

## By the numbers

| 300K+ | 1.2M+ | 5 channels |
|---|---|---|
| **survey responses** through Fabrikam Desk in 12 months: **the inner loop** | **Fabrikam Desk tickets** imported into Example Feedback Feedback Library in the same 12 months: **the outer loop** | Surveys were delivered via web, in-product, mobile app, automated Fabrikam Desk messages and email. |

## Inner loop and outer loop

Every Voice of Customer program runs two loops. Example Feedback's Fabrikam Desk integration covers both.

| Loop | What it fixes | Where it runs in Example Feedback and Fabrikam Desk |
|---|---|---|
| **Inner loop** | One customer's problem, on their own ticket | An Example Feedback survey goes out from Fabrikam Desk, and the answer comes back to the same ticket for an agent to act on |
| **Outer loop** | The pattern behind many feedback sources, for every customer it affects | Fabrikam Desk tickets and survey responses are centralized in Feedback Library, where the Theme Agent finds recurring themes across sources |

The inner loop closes a ticket well. The outer loop makes sure the same ticket doesn't come back next month.

## The three use cases, by the numbers

| Use case | Loop | What happens | Last 12 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Triggered surveys | Inner | A Fabrikam Desk event, such as a ticket being solved, sends an Example Feedback survey to the customer | 241,318 responses |
| 2. Survey answers back to Fabrikam Desk | Inner | An Example Feedback automation sends the response to Fabrikam Desk: it updates the ticket, adds a comment, or opens a new ticket | 198,764 responses |

Use cases 1 and 2 often run on the same survey, so their totals overlap. Together they add up to 327,905 unique responses tied to a Fabrikam Desk ticket.

## The problem every CX team knows

A customer who gave you a 5 in March is a 1 in May. One late delivery, one dropped consultation, one refund that took a week. With no subscription to cancel, churn looks like this: they order less, then stop.

The survey caught it, but the score went to a dashboard the agent never saw, and nobody went back to the customer. The inner loop never closes, and with nobody connecting the dots across tickets, the outer loop never starts.

## The shift: feedback goes out with the ticket, and comes back to it

Often it's the same survey: Fabrikam Desk sends it, the customer answers, and the answer lands back on the ticket it came from. Nobody switches tools or exports anything. And because every answer belongs to a known customer and ticket, the same complaint across one market, plan or issue type becomes a pattern you can fix once for everyone. That's the outer loop, starting from the same data.

> "We rebuilt our VoC program around the customer journey, and now we ask fewer questions and get clearer answers."
>
> Alex Morgan, Senior VoC Program Manager, Northwind Traders

## How each use case works

### 1. Surveys triggered by Fabrikam Desk events

A Fabrikam Desk automation sends the email after a ticket is solved. The first question of an Example Feedback survey is embedded in the email, so the customer answers without leaving their inbox. Each answer is tied to that customer and that ticket, which turns CSAT into a transactional metric you can cut by issue type, brand or market.

{screenshot}

### 2. Survey answers back to Fabrikam Desk

After the score, the customer explains it in an open question. That answer goes back to Fabrikam Desk as a private comment on the original ticket. A low score can also change the ticket status, raise its priority or open a new ticket, on conditions the CX team sets. The agent sees the feedback where they already work, and the inner loop closes without anyone exporting anything.

{screenshot}

### 3. Feedback centralization

Help desk tickets are one of the richest feedback sources a company has, and usually the least analyzed. Synced into Feedback Library, they sit next to survey responses, so CX and research teams centralize feedback instead of reading each tool separately. Fabrikam Desk now makes up 18.6% of all notes the Theme Agent reads, second only to Example Feedback surveys. A project across tickets and survey responses shows how often an issue really comes up, with every finding linked to the ticket it came from.

{screenshot}

## Where it's happening: B2C software and apps

B2C software and apps sent 162,440 responses through the integration in the window. Volume is high, customers don't have account managers, and a bad interaction quietly turns into a lost user.

**Consumer apps** live on volume: large user bases, high ticket counts and no account manager to notice when someone drifts away. They use the inner loop end to end. A CSAT question goes out after every support contact, and an unhappy user's open answer comes back to the ticket so an agent can follow up.

**Fintech and banking apps** are 12% of the cohort, and 94% of their responses came from surveys sent through Fabrikam Desk. For them use case 1 is the core: transactional CSAT after every support contact, by issue type, brand or market, without a second tool.

**Healthcare software and apps** are 17%, and 81% of their responses went back to the ticket. For them use case 2 is the core: a bad experience after a visit or consultation lands on the ticket as a comment, and the patient experience team sees it the same day.

## How to set it up

1. [Send a survey after solving a ticket](https://help.example.com/articles/fabrikam-desk-survey-after-ticket) (use case 1): create an email survey, pick Fabrikam Desk under Identify respondents, add the attributes you'll segment by, and drop the embedded first question into a Fabrikam Desk automation.
2. [Connect the survey to Fabrikam Desk](https://help.example.com/articles/fabrikam-desk-integration) (use case 2): choose whether responses update tickets, add comments or create new ones, and set the conditions for each.
3. [Sync tickets into Feedback Library](https://help.example.com/articles/fabrikam-desk-feedback-library) (use case 3): add Fabrikam Desk as a feedback source, so tickets and surveys are analyzed together.
