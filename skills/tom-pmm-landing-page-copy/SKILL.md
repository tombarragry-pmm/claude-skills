# Landing Page Copy Skill

The examples use Example Feedback, a fictional customer feedback platform, and its fictional research product, Feedback Lab. Replace the product names, feature names and taglines with your own.

## How We Work

Always go section by section. Never rewrite the full page speculatively.

For each section:
1. Present the current copy
2. Give an honest take on what is weak and why
3. Suggest directions or angles before drafting full copy
4. Agree on the angle first, then refine body and bullets
5. Offer multiple short options for the user to react to and hybridize

Never jump straight to a full draft. Agree on direction first.

---

## Page Structure

Standard section order for Example Feedback product/feature landing pages:

1. Hero (Eyebrow, H1, Subhead, CTAs)
2. Logo banner / Trusted by
3. Feature Section 1
4. Feature Section 2
5. Feature Section 3
6. Customer testimonial
7. Capability cards (x6)
8. How it works (Collect, Analyze, Act)
9. AI accordion (where relevant)
10. FAQ
11. Closing CTA

Feature sections should suggest a workflow the persona actually uses, not just list features. Think: what does this person do on a Tuesday, and where does this feature live in that day?

## Section-by-Section Guide

### Hero

**Eyebrow:** Product or category label (e.g. "Feedback Lab" or "Feedback Lab: Research Repository"). Carries the category so the H1 doesn't have to.

**H1:**
- Goal: Name the problem and signal the category. Let the subhead carry the feature detail.
- The subhead takes weight off the H1. The H1 does not need to cover everything.
- Avoid cramming category label + value prop into one sentence.
- Strong pattern: contrast format ("X does Y. This one does Z.") or punchy outcome statement.
- Weak patterns to avoid: two jobs in one sentence, vague transformation language.

**Subhead:**
- Covers what the product does in plain terms.
- Should feel like a natural extension of the H1, not a repeat of it.
- Aim for one sentence, outcome-focused.

**CTAs:** Start Free / Book a Call (standard)

**Design direction:**
- Show the journey from raw inputs (scattered tickets, transcripts, reviews) flowing into a clean, polished report on the other side
- The report should feel like a real deliverable, not a dashboard
- Alternative: consider an interactive demo built in your interactive demo tool (e.g. Arcade) showing the end-to-end workflow

---

### Feature Sections (x3)

Each section has: H2, body copy, 2 bullets, CTA

**H2:**
- 4 to 6 words
- Lead with the outcome or the problem, not the feature name
- Should be obviously about what the section covers (not clever at the expense of clarity)

**Body copy:**
- 70 to 90 characters
- Focus on the output or outcome, not the mechanism
- Do not repeat what the H2 already says
- Do not explain how the feature works: show what the user walks away with

**Bullets:**
- Max 50 characters each
- Each bullet should add something new, not repeat the body
- Pair them to cover two distinct angles (e.g. AI doing the work + researcher staying in control)
- Avoid: vague action words ("validate", "manage"), feature documentation language, anything that sounds like a tooltip

**CTA:** Try now for free (standard per section)

**Design direction per section:**
- Feature section 1 (Reports): Show human-AI collaboration. Researcher at the center, AI generating content around them, human clearly directing. Include a graph or data visualization. Avoid anything that looks like AI does everything.
- Feature section 2 (Themes/Patterns): Showcase the themes area with customizable topics. Show source logos around the edges feeding in to illustrate multi-source detection.
- Feature section 3 (Conversational analytics / Ask Assistant): Show a question being asked and a specific citation being expanded with more depth. The scenario is a stakeholder asking a follow-up after a report has been delivered.

---

### Capability Cards (x6)

**Header:** 2 to 3 words
**Subtext:** 50 to 70 characters

Cards should cover the key features of the product. Each card is a feature benefit, not a feature name. They complement the feature sections rather than repeat them.

Example standard set for Feedback Lab:
1. Research Projects
2. AI-generated reports
3. Theme tracking
4. Ask Assistant
5. Your surveys, already included
6. 20+ feedback sources

**Design direction:**
- Clean icon-led cards in a 2x3 or 3x2 grid
- Minimal visual, let the copy do the work
- Consistent card height regardless of subtext length

---

### How It Works

Three-step framework: Collect, Analyze, Act

Each step has: Eyebrow (Collect / Analyze / Act), Heading, Body copy (1 to 2 sentences)

Keep copy tight and outcome-focused. This section is about showing the workflow at a glance, not re-explaining features.

**Design direction:**
- Linear or stepped visual showing the three stages
- Source logos visible in the Collect stage
- Report or output visible in the Act stage

---

### FAQ

Target 6 to 10 questions. Prioritize questions that:
- Come up in sales conversations
- Address researcher skepticism about AI
- Cover plan availability, sharing, and integrations

Example standard FAQ topics for Feedback Lab:
- Security and data privacy
- AI hallucinations and accuracy
- AI transparency (data never trains models)
- Plan availability
- Feedback sources supported
- Report sharing outside Example Feedback

---

### Closing CTA

Short, punchy, action-oriented. Example standard for Feedback Lab: "Fewer spreadsheets. Faster decisions."

**Design direction:**
- Full-width CTA block with strong contrast
- Two buttons: Start Free (primary) / Book a Call (secondary)

## Copy Rules (Apply to Every Section)

**Always:**
- US English spelling and grammar
- Active voice and contractions
- Problem-first framing: answer "what pain does this solve" before "what does this feature do"
- Outcome-focused language: what does the user walk away with?
- Connect back to Collect, Analyze, Act where relevant

**Never:**
- Em dashes or en dashes (firm brand rule, no exceptions)
- AI filler language or robotic patterns
- Internal persona names in external copy (never internal codenames like "Data Detective" or "Roadmap Pilot": use actual job titles)
- Feature-dumping: listing capabilities without context or benefit
- Repeating the H2 in the body copy
- Vague superlatives ("best-in-class", "next-gen", "powerful")
- Passive voice constructions

**Researcher-specific rules:**
- Never imply AI replaces the researcher. Always "AI assists" or "AI handles the grunt work"
- Avoid "agentic" framing: it implies reduced user control
- Verifiability is a key message: every conclusion should trace back to real feedback
- Never reference retired features by name (keep a list of them here, e.g. "Old Feature Name")

---

## Tone Reference

- Confident but grounded: demonstrate expertise with specifics, not vague promises
- Direct: no hedging ("We maintain..." not "We strive to maintain...")
- Conversational but professional: contractions are fine, jargon is not
- Slightly cheeky for social/swag copy; more grounded for landing pages and sales content

---

## Design Direction Format

For each section, provide 2 to 3 short bullet points that tell the designer:
- What the visual should show (not how to design it)
- What the key moment or interaction is
- What to avoid

Keep design notes factual and visual, not aesthetic. "Show a report with citations visible" not "use a clean modern aesthetic".
